How to Calculate Your Freelance Rates Realistically

Setting an accurate rate is one of the most vital tasks for freelancers, agency owners, and independent professionals. Most beginners make the mistake of taking an employee hourly rate and multiplying it directly, ignoring essential business costs.

1. Account for Non-Billable Hours

Unlike full-time employees, freelancers do not spend 40 hours a week on client work. A significant portion of your week is spent on admin, client onboarding, invoicing, marketing, and self-education. Realistically, most freelancers only achieve 20 to 25 billable hours per week.

2. Cover Business Overhead & Taxes

Your rate must cover recurring business expenses such as software subscriptions, hardware depreciation, internet, office rental, accounting fees, and local taxes. Always factor in your self-employment taxes before setting a final quote.

3. Price Based on Value, Not Just Hours

Once you establish your baseline hourly rate using RateCraft, consider transitioning to value-based project rates for experienced work to maximize earning potential.
